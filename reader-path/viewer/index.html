How to read this page

A box is one part of the page as the browser gives it to assistive technology. It shows what a screen reader gets there: the role, the accessible name, the description and the state. Select a box to see its content on the right.

Each path button shows the same boxes in a different view. Structure shows the boxes inside each other. Reading order, Tab order and Headings number the boxes and join them with arrows. Landmarks marks the page regions. Messages shows the message boxes. Gaps shows the boxes that have a gap at the chosen level. Actions and results (what a control does) and the events in Messages are filled by a later version; a view with nothing in this model says "none in this model".

"Now" is what the page gives today. "Goal" is what it should give, and where the goal comes from: a WCAG 2.2 success criterion with its level (A, AA or AAA), or "our rule", the rule of comparable information. The goal level buttons choose which gaps are shown: A, AA (includes A) or AAA (includes both); "Only the chosen level" leaves out the lower levels, so Level AAA then shows the AAA gaps alone. A red dashed box has a gap at the chosen level. The drill down buttons open or close the boxes by depth. "This model" counts what the page has and which boxes each level adds.

The results come from the browser's accessibility tree and from the axe-core rules. A person must confirm them; this page is not an evaluation.
